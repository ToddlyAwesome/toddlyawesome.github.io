Branding / Identity / Sports Design

NBA Team
Rebrands

As a basketball player and avid NBA fan, I approached these rebrands through the lens of someone with real emotional connection — balancing legacy with modernity.

Scroll

Rebranding a professional sports team requires a deep understanding of the team's legacy, fan base, and future aspirations. I analyzed existing branding elements — logos, color schemes, history — to identify what resonates with fans and what could be elevated.

Project Type

Concept Rebrand

Scope

Logo / Color / Jerseys

Approach

Legacy-forward modernization

Tools

Adobe Illustrator / Photoshop

Chapter 01

Logo

01

01 — Logo

Legacy branding,
modern refresh.

For the logos I retained key legacy branding elements while giving each franchise a more modern aesthetic. The goal was never to erase history — it was to build on it with intention.

Every mark was studied for what made it iconic, what had aged poorly, and where there was an opportunity to inject new energy without alienating the fanbase.

Chapter 02

Color & Identity

02

02 — Color & Identity

Rooted in history.
Built for now.

I took the same legacy-first approach to color. Understanding the meaning behind each team's original color choices gave me a proper foundation to build complementary palettes that felt earned, not arbitrary.

Colors carry emotional weight for fans. The challenge was honoring that attachment while creating something that looks sharp across jerseys, digital, merchandise, and arena design.

Chapter 03

Jerseys

03

03 — Jerseys

The best representation
of any franchise.

After establishing the logo and color system, it was time to make the art direction tangible. A team's jersey is arguably their most important design artifact — worn on the court, sold in stores, and worn by fans for decades.

Properly applying the new identity to the uniform required thinking about number placement, trim details, fabric texture, and how the design would read both up close and from the nosebleed seats.

Perspective

Sports branding works when it makes fans feel something. As a player and fan myself, I knew the emotional stakes — and I designed with that connection in mind at every step.
